    Canopy disturbance detrending¶

    A tree that loses a competitor overhead grows faster for decades afterwards. That release is real biology, but it is not climate, and when it survives into a standardized chronology it masquerades as a low-frequency climate signal. Canopy disturbance detrending finds those growth releases and removes them, so that what is left tracks climate more faithfully — and, as a by-product, it hands you the disturbance history itself, which is often the thing a forest ecologist wanted all along.

    dplPy implements the method of Druckenbrod, Cook, Pederson & Martin-Benito (2024), a direct port of Dan Druckenbrod's MATLAB that is validated number-for-number against it (see Fidelity & departures). The surface is three functions: releases to detect, disturbance_detrend to standardize, and disturbance_plot to look.

    How the method works¶

    For each series, in order:

    1. Detect releases by radial-growth averaging (Lorimer & Frelich 1989). A trailing running mean of ring width is compared window-to-window: the percent growth change at a year is the increase from the preceding window's mean to the subsequent window's mean. A run of years whose percent growth change clears a threshold is a release, pinned to its year of maximum change.
    2. Power-transform the series to stabilize its variance (Cook & Peters 1997), so the spline fits that follow are not dominated by the high-growth years.
    3. Remove each release in turn, newest to oldest, by fitting a short age-dependent spline (Melvin et al. 2007) from the release year onward and subtracting it — while the release sustains elevated growth. A release that is only transient is removed just up to the point where growth returns to its pre-release level.
    4. Age-detrend the disturbance-free series with a second, stiffer age-dependent spline.
    5. Re-express in ring-width units. The climate index is the ratio of the disturbance-standardized series to the age trend.

    Detecting releases¶

    releases runs step 1 on its own and returns a tidy table, one row per event:

    import dplpy as dpl

rwl = dpl.readers("mo024.rwl")        # Stahle's Missouri post oak, 61 series
rel = dpl.releases(rwl)
#   series  year  pct_growth_change
#   DEM01A  1915         216.16
#   DEM03A  1809          64.25
#   ...                           (series-then-year order)

    The disturbance history of the stand falls straight out of this table — count the events per decade and you have the release chronology that the summary figure draws. Detection runs on raw ring widths; do not power-transform first, because the threshold is calibrated on raw growth.

    Two parameters set the sensitivity:

    dpl.releases(rwl, threshold=50, window_length=15)   # defaults (recommended)
dpl.releases(rwl, threshold=25, window_length=10)    # more sensitive, more events

    Druckenbrod et al. recommend 50 % over 15 years over the more permissive 25 % / 10 years; the method needs at least 2 * window_length rings in a series.

    Standardizing¶

    disturbance_detrend runs the whole pipeline and, by default, returns the per-series climate index — the same shape and role as detrend output, so it drops straight into a chronology:

    idx = dpl.disturbance_detrend(rwl)          # DataFrame: years x series
crn = dpl.chron(idx)                         # standard chronology

    A single series in returns a single series out. For the full set of intermediate products — the disturbance-standardized series, the age trend, the residual index, the release table, and the averaged chronology behind the summary figure — ask for the dict:

    info = dpl.disturbance_detrend(rwl, return_info=True)
info["index"]                    # the climate index (St = DSt / At)
info["disturbance_standardized"] # DSt: growth with the releases removed
info["age_trend"]                # At
info["averages"]                 # mean width, age trend, Dt, index, sample depth
info["releases"]                 # the detection table

    Looking at the result¶

    Plotting is built so a many-series call never floods you with figures. disturbance_detrend(rwl, plot=True) draws a single summary figure for a whole chronology — the release histogram with sample depth, the mean widths with the disturbance removed and the age trend, and the mean index:

    dpl.disturbance_detrend(rwl, plot=True)         # one summary figure

    To inspect individual cores, name them with disturbance_plot, which draws the three-panel per-series view (detection, disturbance removal, index):

    dpl.disturbance_plot(rwl, series="DEM01A")              # one core
dpl.disturbance_plot(rwl, series=["DEM01A", "DEM07B"])  # a chosen handful
dpl.disturbance_plot(rwl)                                # the summary again

    Passing disturbance_detrend a single Series with plot=True draws that series' three-panel figure, since there is only one series to show.

    Choices you may need to make¶

    Spline stiffness. release_flex=10 (years) sets how closely each release event is followed and age_flex=30 the stiffness of the final age trend. Both are fixed year-lengths, as in the published method.

    The power transform. transform="druckenbrod" (the default) is the method's own transform and reproduces the published results. transform="powt" substitutes dplPy's standard Cook & Peters powt; it departs materially from the published method and is offered only for workflows that prefer the house transform. transform=None works in raw ring-width units with no transform. The transform and its inverse are the same engine as dpl.powt.

    Absent rings. A locally absent ring is recorded as a zero — a real year with no measurable growth, distinct from missing data (NaN). Scattered zeros pass through as zero growth, but a zero in a series that takes the log branch of the power transform would otherwise produce NaN. recode_zeros=True recodes zeros to 0.001 before processing (the same convention as ssf; the input is never modified); left False, a series containing zeros is detected as-is and a warning names it.

    The denominator guard. min_mean=0.01 (mm) keeps a near-zero baseline — a window dominated by absent or recoded rings — from inflating the percent growth change into a spurious release. The default touches only such pathological windows; min_mean=0 reproduces the unguarded method exactly.

    Coming from TRADER or dplR¶

    dplR has no canopy-disturbance detrending. The R package TRADER implements the detection side (several boundary-line and radial-growth-averaging variants); dplPy's releases covers the radial-growth-averaging detector, and disturbance_detrend goes further, removing the releases and returning a climate index — the detrending step TRADER does not do.

    Fidelity¶

    The detector reproduces Druckenbrod's MATLAB release years exactly on his mo024 test set (94 releases across 52 of 61 series), and the full pipeline reproduces the per-series disturbance series, age trend, index and residual to float64 round-off. The age-dependent spline reuses dplPy's ads, which matches Druckenbrod's ADSmelvin to the same tolerance. See Fidelity & departures for the package-wide principle and References for the works behind the method.
